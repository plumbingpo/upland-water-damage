Bathroom Water Damage in Upland

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. Hidden from view, it builds up behind the wall tile and down in the subfloor. We help Upland homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. With thermal imaging and moisture meters, we map what is wet before any demolition, and we open just the sections that will not dry in place.

Signs to Look For

  • Inside the vanity, a base shelf that is stained, soft or out of shape
  • The caulk line at the shower or tub has split, pulled away, or turned black
  • Near the tub or toilet, sheet vinyl that is lifting, curling or turning gray
  • A spongy feel in the floor beside the tub or in front of the vanity
  • A toilet that rocks slightly, or dark staining around its base
  • Tiles near the tub that have loosened, shift when stepped on, or sound hollow

Our Process

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Pinpoint the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Open What Is Needed

Soft wall board, loose tile and subfloor that has rotted are removed. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Seal

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Mold in the Tiled Wall

By themselves, grout and tile do not keep water out. Mold takes hold on the wall board once that hidden layer remains wet, and no scrubbing can get to it.

Rot Under the Toilet

Each flush leaks a small amount once the wax ring fails. The toilet eventually rocks because this category 3 water has rotted the subfloor at the flange.

Poor Ventilation

Without a working exhaust fan, the steam from showers has nowhere to go. That moisture settles on walls and ceilings, feeds mold, and slows the drying of anything that is already wet.

Damage Downstairs

Before dripping, water runs along joists and pipes, which can put the downstairs stain several feet away from the real leak. Its path can cross electrical wiring and ceiling lights.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

First days

Water Slips Through

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

The first several weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Mold starts on the underside of the floor and inside the wall cavity.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

You see darker grout, loosening tiles and blackened caulk. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months and beyond

The Structure Suffers

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.
