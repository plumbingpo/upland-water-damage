Understanding Why Basements Get Wet

Water gets into basements in several ways, and knowing the source is key to picking the right fix. Soaked soil presses on the foundation, and that hydrostatic pressure drives moisture through any crack, joint, or pore in the concrete. Foundation walls can also crack under the lateral push of expanding clay, which gives water a way inside. If the yard slopes the wrong way, gutters are blocked, or drainage is inadequate, rain collects at the foundation and works its way inside. A basement can also be damp without any leak at all, because humid warm air condenses on cool walls and floors.

Waterproofing From the Outside

Long term, nothing works as well as handling the water outside, before it reaches the wall. A crew excavates along the foundation, puts a waterproof membrane coating on the wall, and then installs new exterior drain tile or upgrades what is there. It is the most expensive approach, but it addresses the problem at its source and gives the most complete protection. Regrading is also worth the money, because ground that falls 1 inch or more per foot across the first 6 feet away from the foundation carries water off.

Interior Waterproofing Solutions

Interior waterproofing systems manage water that gets through the foundation instead of keeping it from reaching the foundation. Interior French drain systems collect water along the perimeter of the basement and send it to a sump pump for discharge. During heavy rainfall and outages, a sump system does the work, and a battery backup is the ideal setup. Interior waterproof coatings and sealants give some protection against minor seepage and efflorescence, but they do not replace proper drainage when water intrusion is significant.

Sump Pump Best Practices

For Upland homeowners with sump pumps, proper maintenance is essential. Every three months, fill the pit with water until the float trips and confirm the pump runs. Listen for unusual sounds that could point to mechanical problems. Basements flood most often when the power is out, so add a battery backup system. A backup pump that runs on water pressure adds further redundancy. Make sure the discharge line sends water at least 6 feet from your foundation and has a check valve to prevent backflow.
