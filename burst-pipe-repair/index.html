A burst pipe is one of the most common water damage events a property can face, and potentially one of the most destructive. A single burst pipe can release 4 to 8 gallons of water per minute, which is 240 to 480 gallons per hour flooding through your walls, ceilings and floors. Whether the cause is freezing temperatures, aging plumbing, excessive water pressure, corrosion or physical damage, a burst pipe demands immediate emergency response to limit the damage water does to structural materials, flooring, personal belongings and building systems.

Upland Restoration provides 24/7 emergency response for burst pipe water damage throughout Upland and surrounding areas. Technicians on our team assist with finding and closing the water supply, while licensed plumbers we coordinate with handle a proper plumbing repair. Once the water source is stopped, we immediately begin extraction with truck-mounted and portable equipment, removing standing water from all affected areas as quickly as possible.

After the water is out, we dry every material that took on moisture: finished floors, subflooring, wood framing, insulation and drywall. Burst pipes often damage multiple rooms and multiple levels, because water follows gravity through wall cavities, floor assemblies and ceiling spaces. Our thermal imaging and moisture detection equipment traces water migration paths so every affected area is identified and properly dried, which prevents the mold growth and structural deterioration that hidden moisture causes.

How to Prevent Burst Pipe Repair Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Going away in cold months? Keep the thermostat running to protect the plumbing from freezing
  • During freezing weather, keep faucets dripping and cabinet doors open so heat can circulate
  • Disconnect garden hoses and shut off outdoor faucets before the first freeze
  • Every few years, ask a licensed plumber to look over the system for corrosion and worn parts
  • Know where your main water shutoff valve is and how to operate it quickly
  • Insulate exposed pipes in unheated areas such as attics, garages and crawl spaces

Signs It Is Time for Burst Pipe Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Hearing water move through the house while nothing is turned on
  • Water pressure that falls off abruptly at every fixture
  • Water pooling on floors with no apparent source
  • Rusty or discolored water coming from your taps
  • Damp, moldy odors around sinks, tubs or walls that hide pipes
  • Pipes coated in ice or frost when temperatures drop
  • A spray or steady drip coming off pipework you can see

Burst Pipe Repair: What Homeowners Ask

Sudden, accidental water damage from a burst pipe is covered under most homeowner's policies, usually along with extraction, drying and repairs, subject to your policy terms. Whether the plumbing repair is covered varies from one policy to the next. Our team gives you full documentation to submit with the claim.
In as little as 24 to 48 hours, mold may begin developing. Hidden moisture in wall cavities and under floors is the usual cause. Our commercial dehumidifiers take out 10 to 20 times the moisture a home model can. That power, combined with fast extraction, gives the best chance of drying everything in time.
Plan on 2 to 6 hours for the extraction itself. Structural drying typically takes 3 to 5 days. From start to finish, a job with drywall, flooring and paint work may last 1 to 3 weeks, according to how much was damaged. Each customer receives a detailed schedule for their own job.
Water rarely stays where the pipe broke. Gravity pulls it down through ceiling spaces, floor assemblies and the cavities inside walls. Conduits for wiring and the gaps between joists carry it even farther. We use thermal imaging and moisture meters to trace those paths and find every affected area.
Common culprits include corroded or aging lines, pressure that runs too high, physical damage and freezing weather. Pressure above 80 PSI strains pipes and fittings over time. Old galvanized steel and polybutylene pipes are also more likely to fail without warning. A licensed plumber can inspect your system for wear and corrosion.

Why Burst Pipe Repair Is Not a DIY Job

Burst pipe water damage often extends far beyond what is visible on the surface. Materials get saturated in spots that can stay undiscovered for days or weeks, because the water runs along conduits, joists, floor assemblies and the insides of walls. At that point mold can already be growing out of sight, and the structure may have lost a significant amount of strength. With moisture meters and thermal cameras, trained technicians follow the water's path and pinpoint every affected area right away.

Consumer tools cannot compare with the scale of professional drying and extraction equipment. Truck-mounted extractors remove water hundreds of times faster than a shop vacuum, and commercial dehumidifiers pull 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential units. Mold can begin growing in 24 to 48 hours, so this kind of power and speed matters a great deal.
