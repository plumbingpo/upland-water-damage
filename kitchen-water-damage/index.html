Kitchen Water Damage in Upland

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. For homeowners in Upland, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Particleboard boxes swell fast and stay swollen, but solid wood and plywood frequently dry well. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

What It Looks Like

  • Toe kicks that have swollen, darkened, or pulled away from the cabinet base
  • Opening the sink cabinet releases a stale, damp odor
  • An unexplained jump in water use, or dripping you can hear when nothing is running
  • Warping, staining or a soft spot in the floor of the cabinet below the sink
  • Behind the dishwasher or sink, a wall with staining or blistered paint
  • Downstairs, a brownish mark on the ceiling beneath the kitchen
If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

The first hour

Water Runs Under

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Begin to Swell

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

Laminate swells at the edges, hardwood cups, and vinyl lifts once the adhesive under it fails. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

After a week

Tear Out Likely

Swollen cabinet boxes cannot be brought back. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Stop the Source

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Map the Moisture

The base of each cabinet, the wall in back and flooring all the way across the kitchen are checked with meters and a thermal camera. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry Without Demolition

With the toe kicks off, air gets pushed below the cabinets and behind them. An antimicrobial is applied to damp cavities while air movers and dehumidifiers work over several days.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Wet Wall Behind Cabinets

The wall at the back of the sink and dishwasher holds drain and supply pipes. When they leak, framing and drywall get wet behind the cabinets, and mold spreads where no one can see it.

Toe Kick Space

Every side of the space beneath base cabinets is closed off. It gathers water that sits for weeks, and the resulting mold stays hidden unless the cabinets are removed.

Flooring Over Flooring

It is common for a kitchen floor to be laid right over the previous one. Trapped water sits between the two, with no way to evaporate and no sign on the surface.

Sink Drain Leaks

Because it leaks just when the sink drains, a loose fitting or a worn disposal seal often goes unnoticed. A damp sink cabinet smells and grows mold quickly because that water has food waste in it.
