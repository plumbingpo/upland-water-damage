Of all the problems an owner can have, sewage coming up through the drains ranks among the most dangerous. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. When it happens in Upland, Upland Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Multiple drains in your home backing up at the same time
  • Tubs, toilets or floor drains pushing black water back into the room
  • Soggy, smelly ground over the sewer line or leakage at the cleanout cap
  • Sewage residue visible around drain openings or on basement floors
  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up
  • Strong sewage or rotten egg odors coming from drains or lower levels
  • Carpet or flooring contaminated when a toilet overflowed with solid waste

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open
  • Use drain strainers in showers and sinks to catch hair and debris
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • A backwater valve at the sewer connection stops the city system from backing up into your home
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain

What a Professional Brings to Sewage Cleanup

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.
