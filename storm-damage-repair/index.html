An Upland property can take on a lot of water when a hurricane, a tropical storm, a flash flood or a heavy downpour hits. When rain is blown sideways, it gets in through shattered windows, failed siding and openings in the roof. A single restoration plan from our team covers each of these related issues together.

Maintenance Tips for Storm Damage Repair

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Good attic insulation and ventilation stop ice dams from forming along the roof edge
  • Branches break in high wind, so keep nearby trees pruned away from the roof and siding
  • Before storm season each year, get the roof inspected and its problem areas repaired
  • Reseal worn caulk and weatherstripping around windows and doors before the rainy season
  • Know how to shut off your main water supply in case a storm damages a pipe
  • Clogged gutters overflow in a downpour, so clear them and the downspouts often

Why Storm Damage Repair Is Not a DIY Job

Because one kind of storm damage feeds another, the work needs restoration professionals with real experience. Wet insulation, soaked roof decking and damp wall cavities often sit behind a leak that appears small, and without proper detection and drying they bring mold and a weakening structure. Professional restoration technicians use thermal imaging and moisture meters to trace water paths and identify every affected area.

Temporary protection such as tarps and board-up needs correct technique or it will not hold. If a tarp is fastened poorly, it can direct water where you do not want it or give out when the wind picks up. We have done a lot of storm mitigation, and we use suitable materials and methods so the temporary protection holds until permanent repairs are on the calendar.

Signs It Is Time for Storm Damage Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Bubbling paint and soft, drooping spots overhead once the rain stops
  • Water getting past doors, windows and wall penetrations when wind pushes the rain
  • From ground level, the roof shows shingles torn off, broken or raised
  • Ceilings or walls that stain while it is storming or soon afterward
  • Downspouts knocked off and gutter runs hanging after high wind
  • Wet insulation in the attic or wall cavities found after bad weather
  • A musty smell indoors in the days after heavy rain
