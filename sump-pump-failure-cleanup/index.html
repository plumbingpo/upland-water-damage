Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Upland

The moment a basement most needs its sump pump is often the moment the pump gives out. Heavy rain raises the water table, the pit fills, and then the power goes out, the float sticks or the pump cannot keep up. The pit overflows and the basement floor starts to flood. Until pumping resumes, the water continues to rise.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. Having passed through the ground, it usually holds bacteria, silt and drain tile residue, which makes it Category 2 at best, or Category 3 once it has been standing or sewage has mixed in. For the job, this means soaked porous items such as drywall and carpet pad are usually discarded, and what stays gets washed and disinfected first, then dried.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stay Out of the WaterWith the electricity still on, a flooded basement is not safe to walk into. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerIf the breaker panel can be reached while you stand somewhere dry, switch off the circuits for the basement. A panel surrounded by water is a job for the power company.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyLook to see whether the pump came unplugged, its outlet tripped or the float is pinned against the pit wall. Keep your hands out of the water.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedWear rubber boots and gloves if you must go down, keep kids and pets upstairs, and wash your hands after touching anything wet.
  5. Move Valuables and CallCarry dry valuables upstairs, photograph the water level and the pump, then call us. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
Insurance

Insurance and Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

Sump pump losses are widely misunderstood when it comes to insurance. Standard homeowners coverage generally leaves out sump overflow and backup, while flood coverage is bought separately and follows other rules. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Check the declarations page for it, or call your agent to find out. To keep the claim file complete, our team documents the damage, the failed pump and the height of the water.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Losing electricity in a storm when the pump has no battery backup.
  • Motor failure in a pump that has simply reached the end of its life.
  • A pump too small for the volume of water entering the pit.
  • Water returning down the discharge pipe because the check valve no longer seals.
  • A tripped outlet or breaker, or a plug knocked loose, leaving the pump without power.

What Happens if You Wait

Bottom Plates and Studs

Framing lumber at floor level takes on groundwater before anything else in the wall. With the wall opened up, this wood is scrubbed, treated and then dried to normal readings.

Carpet and Flooring

There is no safe way to clean pad and carpet that sat in groundwater, so both are removed. Laminate and vinyl plank trap silty water underneath and usually come up too.

Drywall and Wall Insulation

The wet zone inside a wall reaches well past the water mark, as drywall and insulation pull moisture up off the floor. The lower part that got wet is cut away, not dried, on account of the contamination.

Outlets and Wiring

Rising water can get into receptacles mounted low on basement walls. Have an electrician look at wet receptacles and cable before power is restored to them.

How We Fix It

Our Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Process

1

Pump Out the Basement

Because the pumps and extraction units are ours, the broken sump does not hold up the work. Low spots, the area by the pit and the rest of the floor are cleared of water and silt.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Our team wears protective gear to remove wet drywall, insulation, carpet and pad reached by groundwater, then bags and hauls it off, leaving bare framing and concrete.

3

Wash and Disinfect

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. This happens before drying so contaminants are not blown around the basement.

4

Dry and Verify

Typically for 3 to 5 days, air movers and dehumidifiers stay in place, until the concrete and framing read normal on a moisture meter. A working pump should be back in the pit before our equipment leaves.
