Dealing With Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Upland

An overflowing toilet seems like something towels can handle, except the water keeps moving past the tile. Some slips beneath the baseboard and into the wall, and some drops through the opening around the drain pipe. What matters most is what was in it. If it came from the tank or the supply line, it is clean. Anything that rose out of the bowl is a different matter and must be treated as contaminated.

Our Upland crew starts by identifying the water category, because that sets the rules for everything after. If it is clean, we extract it and dry the area. Black or gray water calls for protective equipment, taking out porous materials that got soaked, carpet pad for example, and disinfecting everything it contacted. After that, moisture meters go on the wall bases, the subfloor and the room below to make sure no wet spot remains.

Your First Steps

  1. Stop the WaterFind the shutoff valve behind the toilet and turn it clockwise as far as it goes. When the valve is frozen, remove the lid of the tank and hold the float up so filling stops.
  2. Stop FlushingDo not flush again or run sinks, showers or the washer. With a blocked drain line, whatever goes down a drain may return through the toilet.
  3. Avoid Dirty WaterIf the water came from the bowl or contains waste, keep children and pets out, and wear gloves and waterproof shoes if you must walk through it.
  4. Check the Room BelowIf the toilet is upstairs, go check the ceiling directly below it. A ceiling that sags or drips means turning off that room's power and moving your things clear.
  5. Block the SpreadUse old towels to keep clean water from reaching carpet or hallways. Anything contaminated is a job for people with proper gear and disinfecting products.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Fill valves or flappers that stick, so the tank never stops running.
  • A main sewer line blockage that backs waste up through the lowest toilet.
  • Waste water with nowhere to go because the septic tank is full or the drain field is saturated.
  • Tree roots or a collapsed section in the sewer line that slows every drain in the house.
  • A cracked tank or failed supply line that floods the bathroom with clean water.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Odor That Returns

An odor can return every time the weather turns humid if contaminated water dried beneath the floor or inside the bottom of a wall, and only removing the affected material ends it.

Soaked Subfloor

Water gets beneath tile and vinyl through grout lines and the toilet base. The plywood underneath swells, delaminates and can loosen the flooring bonded to it.

Swollen Baseboards and Trim

Sitting at floor level, door casings and baseboards wick water up from their lower edge. Fiberboard trim, the most common kind, swells and splits and never returns to shape.

Bacteria in Building Materials

Bowl and drain water leaves bacteria in carpet, pad, baseboards and drywall. Porous materials cannot be reliably disinfected and usually have to be removed.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

A sudden and accidental overflow, for example from an unexpected clog or a fill valve that failed, is covered by many homeowners policies. Unless a backup endorsement was added, policies often exclude water backing up out of a drain or the sewer, which may include an overflow that a blocked main line caused. Damage from a toilet that leaked at the base for months is usually not covered. To learn which of these applies in your case, check the policy or phone your agent.
