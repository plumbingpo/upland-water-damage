Put simply, mitigation is the work of holding damage to its current size. Repair is a different job. Mitigation covers the steps taken ahead of repairs so undamaged materials stay that way. Our Upland crew mitigates a loss in four moves: control the source, extract the water, contain the wet zone and stabilize the building. When this stage goes right, there is less to rebuild afterward, occasionally nothing at all.

The words mitigation and restoration get used as if they were interchangeable. They are two phases. Mitigation is the emergency phase: stop the loss, limit the spread, stabilize the home. Afterward comes restoration, when dryness is verified and ruined flooring, drywall and finishes get fixed or rebuilt. Insurance companies also bill the two as separate line items, which makes it useful to know your current phase.

On a standard mitigation call we work through four items. One: control the source and cut off any new water. Extraction, so standing water is gone. Three: contain the area with plastic sheeting and managed airflow, so clean rooms stay free of moisture and contaminants. Stabilization, which means removing materials that cannot be saved, treating surfaces with antimicrobial, and setting air movers and dehumidifiers. Along the way, your belongings and furniture get moved or set up on blocks to keep them safe.

The Benefits of Professional Water Damage Mitigation

Doing mitigation well means making a string of quick judgment calls. What drywall stays and dries where it is, and what must be removed? Which category applies, 1, 2 or 3, and did that change after the leak first began? Is removing the cabinet toe kick necessary? Our team makes these calls from training and from moisture readings, not from how a surface looks or feels to the hand.

Good mitigation also protects your claim. Under most policies you must act reasonably to keep the damage from growing, with proof that you did. On arrival we take photos, then log moisture readings every day and itemize whatever is removed. The result is a file documenting both the damage and the steps taken. Whoever handles repairs then begins from a known condition, and nothing wet ends up sealed behind new wallboard.

Water Damage Mitigation Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Before heavy rain returns, caulk openings at doors, windows and roof penetrations.
  • Clean the gutters regularly, and aim downspouts so they discharge away from the foundation.
  • Once a month, look beneath sinks and near toilets for stains, drips or soft spots in the floor.
  • Get the water heater checked every year, and replace it ahead of a tank failure.
  • Have an automatic shutoff fitted, so the main closes itself once a leak is sensed.
  • Learn where your main shutoff is and confirm the valve still turns.

What to Expect From Our Water Damage Mitigation

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's what you can expect when you choose Upland Restoration:

1

Stop the Water Source

Depending on the cause, our crew closes the supply, caps a failed line, or covers the entry point with tarps and boards. When the permanent repair calls for a roofer or plumber, we help schedule it.

2

Remove Standing Water

Pumps and extractors take out the standing water, and we inspect the carpet and pad. Drying machines on their own cannot limit damage the way prompt bulk extraction does.

3

Contain Wet Areas

Wet rooms are closed off from dry ones with barriers of plastic sheeting and controlled airflow. That way dust, humidity and contaminants do not move through the home during the work.

4

Stabilize and Dry

Materials past saving come out, an antimicrobial is applied to surfaces, and air movers plus dehumidifiers go in. Moisture readings are recorded on every visit until the structure can be repaired, typically in 3 to 5 days.

How to Tell You Need Water Damage Mitigation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • The house feels muggy and window glass is wet with condensation since the leak.
  • Your insurer has told you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage.
  • One day on, damp materials are beginning to smell musty.
  • An opening in the roof or at a window lets rainwater enter the building.
  • Water is moving from one room into the next along the flooring.
  • Baseboards are swelling or pulling away from the wall after a leak.
  • Water went beneath kitchen or bath cabinets, out of reach of towels and fans.
